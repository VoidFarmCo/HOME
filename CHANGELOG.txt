Pueo - changelog
================

Firmware for a handheld multi-radio field tool on the ESP32-2432S028R.
Forked from CiferTech's ESP32-DIV at 90f7967, 2026-09-01.

Features inherited from upstream are inherited, not audited. Where one has
been read closely it is said so in the entry that did it.

Digests for every release, pruned ones included, are at the end of
this file.


0.4.8 - 2026-09-22
------------------

The screen Fast Pair is for, and the two checks that found it.

  * Fast Pair's device list drew three lines to a row at y, y + 11 and
    y + 21, each clearing an 11 px band, inside a row that is 54 px on the
    3.5". Those are font-1 numbers under a font-2 glyph: the lines landed on
    top of each other and every redraw left the bottom half of the old text
    behind. It is the main screen of the feature, and it shipped that way in
    0.4.7.

    0.4.7 moved the list screens to font 2 and fixed three screens that the
    move broke -- Fast Pair's confirmation page, its result page, and Hunt's
    gauge readout. This was the fourth, and the reason it was missed is that
    all four were found by looking rather than by checking.

    The file's own comment said "three lines to a row, same shape as
    Surveillance" while it carried only the first of the three numbers that
    shape is made of. It has kLine2 and kLine3 now, the same values
    Spotter.cpp uses. The 2.8" third line stays at 21 rather than 2 * 11:
    22 plus a 10 px band does not fit a 30 px row.

  * Hunt's picker had it too, in the state you see before anything is found.
    "listening..." and the list of tracker families were 14 px apart under a
    16 px font, so they touched.

  * Hunt's gauge was reading "7 dBm    best -45    143 se". TFT_eSPI does not
    clip and does not wrap: a line wider than the panel is drawn anyway, off
    both edges, and what survives is the middle. That line is 31 characters
    and 0.4.7 doubled its glyphs, which made it 372 px on a 320 px screen.
    The LOST screen had it worse -- "changed address -- Exit and re-pick" is
    34 characters, 408 px.

    Both are rewrapped onto lines that fit. It took making the renderer
    honest to see either of them: the firmware was perfectly happy, and the
    screenshot on this site was drawn at the old size and looked right.

  * tools/check_text_pitch.py. Inside a function that sets PUEO_BODY_FONT, a
    vertical measurement cannot be a literal -- no literal is right for both
    a 16 px font and an 8 px one. It flags y += N, a trailing + N in a
    uiShowLine or drawString y argument, and a clearing band under 16 px. An
    absolute position is left alone, and so is whitespace under 10 px, which
    is below the smaller panel's own line pitch.

    Ten flagged on the first run, nine of them not known about.

    Two things it has to do besides find them. Commas and parentheses inside
    a string are not punctuation, and counting them as such put a fragment of
    a caption where a y argument belonged and skipped the line entirely --
    that hid one of the ten. And every place the font is set has to land
    inside a function the scanner recognised, or a whole screen has left its
    scope and it reports green over nothing.

  * tools/check_text_fits.py. Font 1 advances 6 px per character whatever the
    glyph, so a centred line's width is 6 * size * length and the only work
    is knowing the length -- half of these are snprintf'd, so the format is
    what exists in the source and the widest thing it can produce is what has
    to fit. The assumed widths for %d and the rest are printed with any
    failure, because a check whose assumptions are invisible is one nobody
    can argue with on the day it is wrong.

    It is deliberately not bounding %d by the type. A plain int is eleven
    characters and nothing on these screens is ever eleven characters; the
    grid-label check already learned once that measuring with the wrong
    assumption sends somebody off to shorten text that fits.

  * check_render_sync.py now covers the list screens, which its own commit
    message said it did not. Two drifts, immediately.

    The renderer drew the third line of a row at twice the line pitch, which
    is right on the 3.5" and a pixel low on the 2.8". And the Hunt gauge's
    distance readout follows PUEO_BODY_SIZE on the board and was hard-coded
    to size 1 in the renderer -- so the screenshot on this site showed that
    line at half the height the device draws it.

    It also had a hole: #define NAME <value> was anchored to end of line, so
    the three body constants that carry an explanatory comment were invisible
    to it. A check cannot compare what it cannot see, and it said nothing
    while not looking.

    21 comparisons to 46. Some of the new ones are not comparisons at all but
    things the numbers have to be on their own -- a band at least as tall as
    the glyph it clears, a pitch at least as tall as the band, a third line
    whose text ends inside its row.


0.4.7 - 2026-09-21
------------------

Text you can read on the 3.5" panel, and two checks that would have caught
the ways this went wrong.

  * The list screens use font 2 on the 3.5". That panel is the denser of the
    two, not the looser: 577 px across a 3.5 inch diagonal is 165 ppi
    against the 2.8"'s 143, so the same glyph in the same pixels comes out
    13% SMALLER on the bigger screen. Font 1 at size 1 was a 1.23 mm cap
    height there -- about three and a half point.

    What the extra pixels had bought was rows rather than legibility.
    Surveillance showed thirteen on the 3.5" against eight on the 2.8", all
    of it text you cannot read at arm's length. It shows eight now, at
    2.47 mm, which is what the other panel shows anyway.

    Surveillance, Hunt and Fast Pair follow PUEO_BODY_FONT, PUEO_BODY_LINE,
    PUEO_BODY_GAP and PUEO_BODY_SIZE in shared.h -- the same
    panel-conditional shape PUEO_TILE_ICON and PUEO_STATUS_TALL already had.
    The drone detector deliberately does not: four lines per aircraft at
    this size takes it from ten rows to five against a six-aircraft table,
    and there the count is the information.

  * That change broke three screens on its way in, and all three were the
    same mistake: a bigger font with the old line pitch left under it.
    Fast Pair's confirmation page, its result page and Hunt's gauge readout
    all drew 16 px glyphs at an 11 px advance, or left one line at size 1
    beside a picker at font 2.

    They were found one at a time, by three different means -- reading the
    file, reading further, and somebody looking at the board. The last one
    is the reason the fix is now every hard-coded advance in FastPairScan.cpp
    rather than the lines that were reported.

  * tools/check_render_sync.py. render_screens.py replays the firmware's
    drawing calls at the firmware's own coordinates, which is what makes the
    screenshots on the website evidence rather than illustration -- and it
    cannot include the firmware's headers, so it carries a copy. Nothing
    said when the copy drifted. 21 numbers compared across both panels;
    ten mutants, half of them mutating the renderer rather than the
    firmware, because drift has two directions.

  * check_menu_tables.py measures grid labels against their tiles. TFT_eSPI
    does not clip, so a label wider than its tile draws over its neighbour,
    and nothing was checking. "Drone Detector" is 95 px in the 2.8" panel's
    100 px tile: one character of headroom on a panel nobody has booted.

    Measured against TFT_eSPI's own font table rather than an average
    character width, which mattered immediately -- at 8 px/char the same two
    labels measured 112 and 96 px and both looked like overflows. Both fit.
    An estimate would have had somebody shortening labels that were fine.

  * make_release.sh --publish-only. Every release until now was built twice,
    once to verify and once with --force to publish, and because the script
    is inside its own archive the second zip has a different digest from the
    one that was verified. The verification then described a file nobody
    would download, and had to be redone by hand against the published one.

  * Two back rows were naming the wrong destination. RFID and GPS both read
    "Back to Main Menu" and both return to More, one level up. The wording
    looked inconsistent; the destination was wrong.

  * The skimmer detector's mode line shouts two of its eight modes. WIFI and
    ZIGBEE were cased as their enum names beside Bluetooth and Video. The
    WiGLE exporter's "WIFI" is left alone -- that one is the file format.


0.4.6 - 2026-09-21
------------------

Two corrections. One to the enclosure, and one to a line in 0.4.5's own
notes that was already wrong when it was written.

  * The 0.4.5 entry says the SD slot in the 3.5" lid has not been
    printed. It was printed within the hour of that cut, would not
    take a card, and is fixed. The entry is left standing rather than
    quietly edited: a published release's notes are a record of what was
    believed at the cut, and revising them afterwards is how a changelog
    stops being evidence of anything. This is the correction, in the place
    corrections go -- the same way 0.4.3 carried the correction to 0.4.0.

  * The lid screw boss that was standing in front of the slot. The middle of
    the three lid screws on that side sat at y = 5, which is nowhere near
    the board -- it is outboard of the PCB entirely -- but squarely across
    the 15 mm of gap a card crosses between the wall and the socket mouth.
    8 mm of clear span for an 11 mm card.

    It took two goes. The first moved it DOWN, to sit just below the slot,
    on the argument that a screw beside the opening stiffens the edge a
    16 mm hole weakens and that the spans come out evener -- 77.5 and 71.5
    against 103 and 46. Both true; neither survived the part. Down is
    pinched between the slot and the battery pocket, so it only fits with
    the radius cut, and cutting the radius left the cylinder exactly tangent
    to the wall rather than overlapping it, which is not a 2-manifold, so it
    needed moving outboard as well. Three adjustments holding each other up.
    And it still was not clean: the slot is cut with a prism longer than the
    wall is thick, so it bit a 3 x 0.9 x 4 mm notch out of the corner of the
    boss it had just been necessary to narrow.

    Up needs none of it. Full radius, original x, 2.9 mm clear of the slot
    and 5.4 mm clear of the card, nothing tangent, no notch. The uneven
    spans are the cost and the right one to pay: 103 mm between screws is a
    stiffness argument about a lid with 2.5 mm walls, and what the
    alternative bought was three interacting fudges around a part that has
    to come off a printer.

    A base printed before this needs reprinting. The bosses
    are shared geometry, so the base and the 2.8" lid moved with the 3.5"
    one, and the middle pair of holes will not line up. All four STLs are
    regenerated.

  * Both full-screen alerts place mark-plus-text as one block rather than
    centring the 200 px mark and letting four lines of text fall off the
    bottom of it. Two thirds of the leftover height above, one third below,
    so the block sits low and deliberate -- 133 px and 67 on the 3.5",
    against 110 and 90.

    Derived rather than nudged, because the obvious fixed offset does not
    survive the other panel: the block is 280 px tall, the 3.5" has 200 px
    of slack to place it in and the 2.8" has 40. A constant tuned on one
    runs off the bottom of the other, silently, on the panel nobody has
    booted.

    tools/render_screens.py replays these coordinates and carries the same
    arithmetic, each side naming the other. Two copies of one number, which
    is the shape of most of this repository's worst bugs -- the renderer
    exists precisely so it does not include the firmware's headers, and a
    check comparing the two is worth having the next time this moves.

## No functional change

Nothing in the radio paths, the parsers or the settings moved in this
release. The firmware differs from 0.4.5 only in where two alert screens
draw their contents.


0.4.5 - 2026-09-21
------------------

The drone detector gets a mark of its own and an alert when something turns
up, and everything added over the last three releases has now been raised on
a board rather than argued for.

  * The drone detector opened on Hunt's owl, and had since 0.3.9. A borrowed
    mark on a feature that is not Hunt: nothing would ever fail on it, and it
    told anyone looking at the screen the wrong thing about what they had
    opened. It has its own now -- an owl holding position with its
    sight-lines down on a quadcopter, which is what the feature does. Remote
    ID is a broadcast the aircraft is already making, so the owl is not
    hunting it.

    The rotors are open rings rather than filled discs. A filled circle at
    200 px and one bit per pixel is a blob, and four blobs are not a
    quadcopter.

  * An alert when an aircraft appears, built from the same parts as
    Surveillance's dwell alert: full-screen mark, two rising notes, then a
    fade rather than a cut, because drawBitmap paints only the set bits and
    redrawing the mark in a dimmer colour walks it out without touching
    anything else. Shown once and gone -- a banner that stayed would cover
    the list it is announcing.

    Edge-triggered on the table going from empty to occupied and re-armed
    when it empties, rather than once per aircraft. A busy sky would strobe,
    and the thing worth interrupting somebody for is that there is anything
    up there at all.

    Fired after the queue drain rather than inside ingest(), because a pack
    often carries Basic ID and Location in the same transmission and
    alerting mid-drain would report "ID not broadcast yet" for an aircraft
    whose ID was in the very next message.

    Height and speed, never distance. Remote ID says where the aircraft is,
    not how far away it is, and this device has no position of its own to
    subtract. The last line is gated the way the dwell alert gates its Hunt
    suggestion: the System message carries the operator's own position, so
    that is claimed when it has been received and denied when it has not,
    rather than left as a gap that reads as "no".

  * tools/render_screens.py draws the mark and both alert states, and reads
    the beacon's BeaconArt.h beside icon.h rather than carrying a copy of
    either mark.

## Raised on hardware, not on paper

Everything added since 0.4.2 has now run on a board:

  the dwell alarm, with its Hunt suggestion gated correctly
  Stealth Mode -- every transmitter refused and named itself, every
    listener carried on
  the settings round trip -- "none saved yet" becoming "loaded from SD"
    across a power cycle, on a path that had never once completed since
    the fork began
  per-feature SD logging, writing to a card
  the drone alert, against the beacon transmitting Remote ID

The stealth result is a check of the gates rather than of the radios: it
says the firmware declines to call its transmit paths, not that anything
watched the antenna go quiet. The gates are enumerated by a host check, so
the list cannot shrink without failing it.

## Still not

The SD slot in the 3.5" lid has not been printed. Both numbers placing it
come off QDtech's outline drawing and the opening was checked in the STL
rather than by eye, but nobody has held the part.

The 2.8" image has still never been booted -- and it is now the panel that
several of this release's predecessors were designed around without being
seen on: the scrolling settings list, the six screens that were drawing to
its height on the taller panel, and the visible-row count derived from it.


0.4.4 - 2026-09-21
------------------

The bench beacon is a download now, and one number on the enclosure stopped
being a guess.

  * pueo-0.4.4-beacon-35-merged.bin. Every release until this one built the
    detector only, so the beacon shipped as source and building it meant the
    1 GB toolchain -- which is the same reason the detector's merged image
    is offered at all.

    It carries the role in its name rather than only a panel, because it is
    the one artefact here that transmits and "beacon" has to be in the
    filename of anything somebody might flash by reaching for the wrong
    line. 3.5" only: that is the panel the second board is, and an unbooted
    transmitter is a worse thing to publish than an unbooted receiver, which
    fails by finding nothing.

    None of the restraint in it depends on reading anything first. Every
    identifier says PUEO-TEST, every address carries DEAD in its device
    half, transmit power sits at the floor, the splash counts down five
    seconds under THIS BOARD TRANSMITS in red, and it stops itself after
    fifteen minutes.

    The deploy's prune learned the new filename in the same change. A
    pattern it does not know is an artefact that accumulates on the server
    forever, unlinked and still reachable by direct URL -- and of all the
    files here, a stale transmitter is the wrong one to leave lying about.
    Its check pins the pattern count on purpose, so adding one fails the
    check and sends you to redo the arithmetic by hand; it did.

  * The SD slot in the 3.5" lid is dimensioned rather than estimated.
    0.4.3 placed it off a photograph. QDtech's own outline sheet dimensions
    the back, in the chain style the rest of that file already trusts:

        17.87 + 14.54 + 25.48 = 57.89   socket to the bottom edge of the PCB
        101.50 - 57.89        = 43.61   from the top edge
        101.50/2 - 43.61      =  7.14   above the board centre

    against the 6.7 that came off the photo. Both land inside the opening,
    which is what cutting it oversize buys, but one of them is sourced.

    The side is the part that inverts, and worth stating for that reason:
    the socket is on the right of a Back view, and a back view mirrors, so
    it is the left edge looking at the screen. The I2C, SPI and SPEAKER
    headers sit on the other side in both the drawing and a photograph,
    which is the check that the two views agree about handedness rather
    than one of them being flipped.

    Verified in the STL rather than by eye: the opening lands 16 x 4 on the
    -X wall, centred on the socket and on the PCB's back face. The
    published lid is regenerated.

  * The beacon's two screens are drawn by tools/render_screens.py, which
    reads BeaconArt.h beside icon.h rather than carrying a copy of the mark
    -- the rule Emit.cpp already follows for the signatures.

  * tools/deploy-pueo.sh says when --prune suppressed the push. It always
    has: pruning is never part of a push, so that a prune cannot ride along
    with a routine deploy. But `--live --prune` then ends with "done" having
    transferred nothing, and the digest verify passes, because it checks
    files that are on the server and cannot see the ones that never left.


0.4.3 - 2026-09-21
------------------

Settings that survive a reboot, which they have not since the fork, and two
switches that change what the device does with its radios and its card.

  * settingsLoad() was never called on this board. setup() had it inside
    `#if BOARD_HAS_ESP32S3`; a CYD takes the #else, which applied touch
    defaults and printed "settings defaults (v1, SD deferred)". Nothing else
    in the tree called it. Deferred meant never: brightness, theme, accent
    and auto scan were written by Save and read back by nobody, on every
    boot since 0.1.0.

    It was deferred for a real reason -- a boot-time SD.begin() right after
    tft.init() was rebooting the device -- and the cause of that,
    gpio_reset_pin() on the shared SPI pins, is compiled out on this board
    now. The load sits where the S3 branch has always had it, with board
    touch defaults applied first, so a card that will not mount leaves the
    device exactly where it used to be.

    The boot line says which of four things happened: loaded, none saved
    yet, unreadable, or no card. It said "loaded from SD" for a card with no
    settings.json on it, which is the reassuring half of a message that had
    read nothing.

  * Stealth Mode. Receive only. Nineteen features whose job is to transmit
    refuse to start, name themselves and say where the switch is; everything
    that listens keeps listening.

    The part worth reading is what counts as listening. A WiFi scan is ACTIVE
    unless its third argument says otherwise, and all eight calls in this
    tree left it false -- so "scan for networks" was putting a probe request
    carrying this device's MAC on every channel, the wardriver and the
    background scanner included. A BLE scan is active by default too, and
    four calls asked for it; an active scan answers every advertiser it
    hears. Those are made passive under stealth rather than blocked. The
    wardriver's WiGLE upload, which joins an access point, is refused on its
    own while the log keeps being written.

    RFID is one gate for the whole menu: a PN532 reads a card by energising
    a 13.56 MHz field and waiting for an answer, so "read" transmits exactly
    as much as "clone".

    It is not a hardware interlock. The stacks stay up because the receivers
    need them, and an initialised stack is one call from transmitting. The
    claim is worth the list in tools/check_stealth.py, whose last rule is
    the one that keeps it honest: every esp_wifi_80211_tx() in the tree has
    to sit in a namespace whose setup is gated.

  * Log to SD, per feature. Settings > SD Logging holds a master and one
    switch each for Surveillance, the Jam Detector, the Packet Monitor, the
    ESB Sniffer and the Wardriver. A feature logs when both say yes, and
    each says so in its own language when it cannot -- REC reports "log off"
    rather than "no SD" at a card sitting in the slot.

    It does not cover files asked for by name: a saved .sub, a DuckyScript,
    settings.json itself. Those are the point of pressing the button rather
    than a side effect of running the app.

  * A boot password, in Settings > Boot Lock. Asked for before the menu.
    Salted SHA-256, stretched 20000 rounds, in NVS rather than on the card
    -- the card comes out, and the card was not being read at boot anyway.

    It stops someone who picks the device up, and nothing else. The firmware
    reflashes in twenty seconds and esptool reads the flash out without
    asking this code anything; a CYD has no secure boot and no flash
    encryption. BootLock.h says so in the same words.

  * The dwell alarm, which 0.4.2's duration column implied and did not do.
    The first row to pass ten minutes brings up a full-screen mark, the
    device and how long, and two rising notes. Shown once and faded, not
    held: this is a passive monitoring screen and a banner that stayed would
    cover the list.

    The Hunt suggestion is gated to BLE trackers, because Hunt lists nothing
    else and offering it for a plate reader is sending somebody to an empty
    picker. "May be travelling with you" is the strongest claim the hardware
    supports: Pueo has no position of its own and cannot tell a tracker
    moving with you from a beacon you have been sitting beside.

    The 3.5" can make the noise -- GPIO 26 into the onboard amplifier, GPIO
    4 to enable it, active low. Two things keep it quiet and neither is a
    fault: the 2.8" has no pin free for it, and the 3.5" ships with no
    speaker fitted. Upstream's BUZZER_PIN guard was `#ifdef`, which is true
    of the -1 "no buzzer" sentinel, so every board has been calling
    ledcAttachPin(-1, 7).

  * The BLE scan was starving the WiFi one. Surveillance and Drones both
    listen on two radios and there is one radio; a BLE scan whose window
    equals its interval asks for it continuously. Measured with the bench
    beacon, which counts what it sends: 495 ALPR probe requests transmitted,
    first one heard after about five minutes. Now immediate.

  * Six more screens were drawing to a 320 px height on a 480 px panel. Two
    Bluetooth log views showed 26 lines where 40 fit; wifi.cpp's content
    bottom fell back to 320, clipping every screen without a touch nav bar.
    The sweep that fixed SCREEN_WIDTH had missed SCREEN_HEIGHT on adjacent
    lines, because its checks named one and not the other.

  * The Drones list came back blank on re-entry. setup() cleared the craft
    table and the screen and never told the line cache, so every line matched
    what the cache believed was already drawn. forgetDrawn() was written for
    it and never called.

  * The settings screen scrolls, so the row count is no longer capped by the
    shorter panel, and the NeoPixel switch is gone -- it saved, reloaded and
    controlled nothing, on any board, because there is no NeoPixel driver in
    the tree.

  * The lid has a slot for the SD card. Fitting one meant four screws and
    lifting the screen out. Its position came off the outline drawing and a
    photograph rather than calipers, so the opening is cut oversize and the
    two numbers that place it are one line each -- see the header of
    docs/pueo/pueo-enclosure.scad. The published STL is unchanged until they
    are measured.

  * tools/build.sh upload compiles first. It uploaded whatever was last in
    the build directory, which twice in one session was a build from before
    the edit being tested: four verified hashes and a board running the old
    firmware.

  * Three check scripts were missing from the source archive -- written,
    relied on, and left out of every release from 0.3.x to 0.4.2, so those
    archives ship a tree that cannot run its own checks. The cut now fails
    when a tools/check_*.py is not in the include list.

  * Correction to 0.4.0. That entry says uiShowLine() in utils.h "is now the
    only copy" of the cached-line painter. It was not: Surveillance had its
    own, and Hunt still has a centred variant of it. Surveillance was
    converted in this release; Hunt's remains, documented as a variant
    rather than a duplicate. Two copies, not four, and not one.


0.4.2 - 2026-09-21
------------------

A partition table, which is 1.12 MB the sketch did not have.

  * huge_app instead of min_spiffs. min_spiffs splits the flash into two
    1.88 MB app slots so an over-the-air update can be written into whichever
    one is not running. The sketch was at 89% of one of them, which is the
    point at which every new feature turns into an argument about space.
    huge_app gives a single 3.00 MB slot, and the same sketch sits at 55%:
    free space goes from about 213 KB to 1.33 MB.

    The spare slot was buying exactly one thing -- Tools > Update Firmware,
    which copies a .bin off the SD card into it. That came from upstream, is
    not mentioned anywhere in this changelog because nobody here has ever run
    it, and duplicates what a USB cable does in twenty seconds.

    It is not removed. Removing the menu entry means renumbering the three
    below it and their self-pinning assignments, which is the exact shape of
    bug that nearly shipped in 0.4.0, for about 15 KB against a 1.12 MB gain.
    It checks for a second slot and says why it cannot run, which is better
    than Update.begin() failing with a number.

  * Nothing else moves, and that was checked rather than assumed. NVS keeps
    its offset in both tables, so settings survive; the merged image rewrites
    the partition table at 0x8000, so flashing it is the whole migration; and
    the image was flashed to a board, which booted, printed its banner and
    loaded its settings. The merged binary was then read back at 0x8000 to
    confirm it carries a 3.00 MB app0.

    If you are updating from 0.4.1 or earlier, flash the merged image as
    usual. There is nothing extra to do.


0.4.1 - 2026-09-21
------------------

Every detector in this firmware has now been shown to receive something.
That sentence could not be written before 0.4.0, and writing it turned up
two more faults -- one here, one in the bench beacon.

  * Hunt's picker never repainted when a tracker arrived. record() updates
    the target list and did not set the dirty flag; enterPicker() sets it
    once, at entry, while the list is empty, and after that only a button
    press does. So the picker drew "no trackers" and stayed that way however
    many were in range, while the gauge worked perfectly -- its branch sets
    the flag every pass for the live needle.

    Two things hid it. Scrolling revealed the list, because a button press
    sets the flag, so it read as "nothing found" rather than "not
    repainting". And it only bites when a tracker appears *after* the screen
    is opened; anything already in range at entry shows up in the first
    draw. It took a transmitter that advertises on a schedule to produce
    that ordering reliably.

    Shipped in 0.4.0. A display fault rather than a wrong answer, but the
    screen exists to be read.

  * The beacon gave every BLE decoy its own address. It had been sending all
    five from the ESP32's one MAC, and Spotter keys a row on the MAC and
    keeps the strongest signature that row has matched. The Meta glasses
    pair is Strong and the KARR vehicle name is Likely, so the vehicle match
    was folded into a row labelled Meta Ray-Ban and never got a line of its
    own.

    The detector was right: one device is not credibly both, keeping the
    stronger signature is correct, and it marked the row corroborated to say
    so. The bench was the thing lying, by pretending five pieces of hardware
    were one. Each decoy is now a random static address, C0:50:55:DE:AD:xx.

    Worth recording that the corroboration marker was the tell, and nobody
    read it. It appeared on exactly the row that had absorbed the missing

0.4.0 - 2026-09-21
------------------

A second firmware that transmits what the first one listens for, and the
four bugs that finding it made visible.

  * PueoBeacon. PUEO_ROLE=beacon builds a separate image for a second board.
    It emits Remote ID over Wi-Fi Beacon and BLE, a plate-reader probe, a
    body-camera beacon, smart glasses, a vehicle module, a Find My tracker
    and Fast Pair in both payload shapes -- which is every detector this
    project has.

    It exists because none of those detectors had ever been proved to
    receive anything. They are checked against synthetic frames and, where
    one exists, a reference implementation, and none of that says a radio
    path works: a decoder can be correct while the scan never hands it a
    byte, and the symptom is an empty list, which is also what an empty room
    looks like. Drones shipped in 0.3.9 with no way to tell those apart.

    A separate image, not a menu entry. Surveillance and Drones both say
    they transmit nothing, on screen and on the website, and that is a claim
    about the binary as much as the feature.

    Emit.cpp includes the detector's headers rather than restating the
    constants, because a decoy built from a second copy tests the copy: the
    tables drift, the bench goes green, and the thing it was meant to prove
    is what stopped being true. check_beacon.py, 38 checks, cross-references
    every decoy against the signature tables it is supposed to trip.

    Everything it sends is built to be recognised rather than to be
    convincing -- PUEO-TEST identifiers, DEAD in the device half of every
    address, transmit power at the floor, a five-second countdown before it
    starts and a ten-minute auto-stop. These signals do not stay on a bench
    because that is where the bench is: Remote ID has public receiver apps,
    and a fake plate reader lands in somebody else's anti-surveillance tool
    the same way. The vendor OUIs themselves are real, because an OUI
    matcher cannot be tested without the OUI it matches.

  * A missing CC1101 no longer freezes the device. ELECHOUSE_CC1101's reset
    is `while(digitalRead(MISO_PIN));` -- it waits for the chip to say it is
    ready and waits forever, and there are eight such loops in that library.
    With no module wired MISO floats high and the first one never returns,
    on the main task, so the whole device stops rather than the feature.

    Five entry points reached it with nothing in front of them: Replay
    Attack, Saved Profiles, Sub-GHz Jammer, Sub-GHz Brute and the Jamming
    Detector. All five froze a board with no radio, which is every board
    until the carrier exists. All five now probe with a deadline first and
    say the radio did not answer.

  * Four screens stopped flickering, and there is now one painter instead of
    four copies of it. Hunt, Surveillance, Fast Pair and Drones each cleared
    their list area and redrew every row several times a second. That is
    invisible on an empty list -- which is how all four were written and
    tested -- and a black flash behind every row the moment anything is
    found. It was the same bug four times because it was the same code four
    times; uiShowLine() in utils.h is now the only copy.

  * Spotter is Surveillance, and it and Drone Detector move out of Bluetooth
    into More. Neither is a Bluetooth feature: both listen on Wi-Fi and BLE,
    and Drones was filed there only because Spotter already was. Spotter was
    also the one feature name that did not say what you get, and the list is
    plate readers, body cameras, smart glasses and vehicle modules.

    The namespace stays Spotter. It is an internal identifier and renaming
    it would touch every file for no reader's benefit.

  * Two more 2.8" coordinates found stranded on the 3.5". The corroboration
    marker sat at x=224, mid-screen instead of at the right edge. The More
    grid's touch targets were 100 by 60 against tiles that are 145 by 92, so
    two thirds of every tile in that grid ignored a tap. Both survived the
    panel sweep by looking like arguments rather than like dimensions, which
    is worth remembering the next time that sweep is trusted.


0.3.9 - 2026-09-20
------------------

Drone detection: Broadcast Remote ID, on the two transports this board can
actually receive.

  * Bluetooth > page 2 > Drones. Aircraft required to carry Remote ID
    announce who and where they are in the clear, with no pairing and no
    key, to anybody in range. This listens. It transmits nothing, associates
    with nothing and connects to nothing, which puts it beside Spotter
    rather than beside the jammers.

    Wi-Fi Beacon arrives through a promiscuous handler walking the
    information elements for the ASD-STAN vendor element -- the sibling of
    the walk Spotter already had. BLE 4 legacy arrives through a passive
    scan reading service data under 0xFFFA, the same shape FastPairScan
    reads under 0xFE2C. Neither receiver is new; only the decode is.

  * What it cannot hear is on the screen, not in a footnote. BLE 5 Long
    Range is the transport a lot of newer aircraft prefer and a classic
    ESP32 has no receiver for it; Wi-Fi NAN is a fourth transport and is not
    implemented. So an empty list means "nothing on Wi-Fi Beacon or BLE 4
    legacy", which is a narrower claim than "no drones", and the header says
    so rather than leaving it to be inferred from a blank screen.

  * DroneId.{h,cpp} knows nothing about radios or screens. Every constant
    and every scale factor in it was taken from the opendroneid-core-c
    reference implementation rather than from memory: the transport
    constants from libopendroneid/wifi.c and transmitter-linux's
    bluetooth.c, the message layouts and decode arithmetic from
    libopendroneid/opendroneid.{h,c}.

    That mattered more than usual here. The scale factors fail quietly.
    Latitude is an int32 of ten-millionths, altitude is a half-metre step
    with a 1000 m offset, and horizontal speed is either quarter-metres or
    three-quarter-metres depending on one bit in a different byte. Get any
    of them wrong and the screen shows a plausible number for a real
    aircraft -- 4000 m instead of 1000, 60 m/s instead of 20 -- with nothing
    about the output to say it is wrong.

  * tools/check_droneid.py, 51 checks. It transcribes the arithmetic
    independently and runs it against vectors with known answers, then feeds
    the framing things that lie about their own length: a message pack
    claiming nine 25-byte messages inside a frame holding one, an element
    declaring more than the frame contains, a 200-byte element size. Then
    120,000 random frames, half of them behind a valid header so the decoder
    is entered rather than rejected at the first byte -- the mistake
    check_nav_labels made, where a rule that never ran reported as passing.
    Every read goes through a buffer that refuses to look outside the frame.

  * Absent fields are not reported as zero. The standard has sentinels for
    "not being sent" and they are honoured: speed 0xFF, direction 255,
    timestamp 0xFFFF. A position of exactly 0,0 is treated as unset rather
    than as a fix, because 0,0 is in the Gulf of Guinea and putting an
    aircraft there is worse than showing no position at all.

  * The promiscuous callback does no decoding. It copies the candidate
    element into a small queue and returns; the arithmetic runs in loop().
    Spotter's capture path is built the same way for the same reason.

  * docs/pueo/enclosure-logo.md loses the test-tile section, which described
    an object withdrawn in 0.3.8, and its "nothing here has been printed"
    note, which stopped being true when the lid was printed and the owl came
    out legible at 30 mm.

0.3.4 - 2026-09-20
------------------

The first release built after any of this ran on a board, and the first to
ship two flash images.

  * It boots, draws and responds to touch. Nothing is wired to it yet, so
    every radio claim in here is still untested and stays marked that way.

  * Two merged images, because the board that arrived was not the board this
    targets. pueo-0.3.4-merged.bin is the 2.8" ESP32-2432S028R, the same
    thing that name has meant since 0.1.0. pueo-0.3.4-35-merged.bin is the
    3.5" ESP32-3248S035R: ST7796 instead of ILI9341, 320x480 instead of
    240x320, backlight on GPIO 27 instead of 21, CC1101 chip select moved to
    21 to get off it, and an XPT2046 hanging off the display's own SPI bus
    rather than the separate T_CLK/T_DIN/T_OUT trio.

    Flashing the wrong one is a dark screen, not an error. There is no
    runtime detection and this release does not pretend there is; the panel
    is chosen at compile time by PUEO_PANEL_35, which tools/build.sh now
    exposes as PUEO_PANEL=28 or PUEO_PANEL=35, with a separate build
    directory each so arduino-cli cannot reuse the other panel's objects.

    The first 2.8" image cut this way was wrong twice over, and neither
    fault would have announced itself:

      Libraries/User_Setup cyd.h still said ST7796 at 320x480 with the
      backlight on 27. PUEO_PANEL_35 reached the sketch's own macros and
      stopped there, so the "2.8" build was an ILI9341 panel driven with the
      3.5"'s driver, dimensions and backlight pin. It branches on
      PUEO_PANEL_35 now -- the define arrives as a -D, so the library's
      configuration header sees the same value the sketch does -- and the
      six settings that differ sit in one block at the top rather than
      scattered through the vendor sections, which is how five of six came
      to be edited at bring-up.

      TOUCH_SHARES_TFT_SPI was 1 for both boards, under a comment explaining
      why the 2.8" needs 0. That build would have driven touch through the
      display's bus, where the 2.8"'s controller is not. It follows the
      panel now.

    tools/check_pinmap.py reads User_Setup through the same small
    preprocessor it uses on the sketch headers, and checks both panels every
    run. Scanning the file line by line was fine while it described one
    board; against a branch it took whichever TFT_BL came first, which
    reported CC1101's chip select as colliding on the 3.5" and clear on the
    2.8" when the truth is the other way round.

    The calibrator advanced the instant readTouchRawXY returned true, with
    no wait for release, so one press satisfied all four targets in
    milliseconds. The first real run returned (3658,3701) (3635,3705)
    (3636,3705) (3551,354) - three of them the same press. It now waits for
    release and takes the median of a nine-sample burst after a settle
    delay.

    Its endpoint arithmetic used min(xs[0],xs[3]) and max(xs[1],xs[2]),
    which assumes raw counts rise with the screen coordinate. On a panel
    where they fall, that picks one reading from each edge pair instead of
    the correct end. It now averages each pair and extrapolates from the
    20 px target inset out to the screen edges, which has no opinion about
    direction.

    mapTouchToScreen chose its Y inversion on defined(BOARD_CYD), which asks
    the wrong question - the two CYDs disagree about Y. It keys on
    TOUCH_INVERT_Y now.

  * utils.cpp named ILI9341_VSCRSADD and ILI9341_VSCRDEF for terminal
    scrolling and would not compile against another driver. 0x33 and 0x37
    are MIPI DCS commands, identical across ILI9341, ST7789 and ST7796, so
    they are named locally and the code no longer cares which panel it is on.

  * Spotter goes from 54 signatures to 99, across eight kinds instead of
    five. Every OUI was checked against the IEEE registry - MA-L, MA-M and
    MA-S - and every service UUID against the Bluetooth SIG's assigned
    numbers, before it went in the table.

    Plate readers gain seven vendor blocks: Motorola Solutions and Genetec,
    both Likely rather than Strong, because both sell a great deal besides
    plate readers and an OUI cannot tell you which product is in range.

    Fixed cameras and doorbells arrive as a new Camera kind, 31 rows: Ring
    (13 blocks), Hikvision, Wyze, Axis, Verkada and Avigilon. These are the
    cameras that are already on the street, as opposed to the ones mounted
    on a pole by a contract.

    A Pentest kind covers a Flipper Zero's own IEEE block, Strong, and two
    Hak5 locally-administered addresses, Weak - an LAA is chosen by the
    device, so it is a hint and never a finding.

    A Tracker kind matches four BLE service UUIDs: Tile's 0xFEED and 0xFEEC
    are Strong, Samsung's 0xFD5A is Likely because SmartTag is one of many
    things behind it, and Eddystone's 0xFEAA is Weak because anyone can
    broadcast one.

    Amazon's own blocks are in at Weak, deliberately: they own Ring, and
    also Echo, and also Kindle.

    outright - E4:05:40 is in none of the three IEEE registries. 00:25:DF is

    panel-aware at bring-up; nothing else was, so on a 3.5" board each app

0.3.3 - 2026-09-20
------------------

Spotter learns to name KARR modules, and the wardriver learns not to upload
them.

  * UC San Diego's "BLE Theft Auto" (USENIX Security 2026) wardrove San
    Diego's highways and found KARR second only to Tesla among BLE devices on
    the road - 608 of 4,314 unique devices - because dealerships fit one to
    every car on the lot. It is a dealer-installed immobiliser and remote
    control whose modules share one hard-coded key across every unit. Two
    models, both advertising a prefix plus exactly eight characters: QT is
    BLE-only, DR adds cellular.

        {"QT ", 11, Kind::Vehicle, Conf::Likely, "KARR BT module"},
        {"DR ", 11, Kind::Vehicle, Conf::Likely, "KARR Cell module"},

    Likely, not Strong, and a module rather than a finding. The shared-key
    vulnerability was patched by the vendor on 2026-07-20, but the fix is
    applied by hand and nothing in the advertisement says whether it has
    been. Presence is what a passive scan can support.

    No OUI rows. The paper reports KARR allocating MACs sequentially inside a
    small set of blocks - 221 outliers in 1,141,798 sampled QT addresses - so
    the addresses are public and corroboration would work, but it plots the
    blocks without printing them.

  * NameSig gains exactLen. It matched on prefix alone, case-insensitively,
    which is fine for "Spectacles" and useless for "DR " - that claims every
    device whose name starts that way, and a match is what puts a row on the
    screen. prefixMatch becomes nameMatch so something reads the field. The
    nine existing rows carry an explicit 0 rather than relying on zero-fill:
    in a signature table, "no constraint" should be visible, not inferred.

  * The WiGLE upload withholds Kind::Vehicle rows. The same paper describes
    the attack beginning with a name-pattern query against a public
    wardriving database - 1,141,798 QT addresses were pulled from WiGLE that
    way - so uploading fresh sightings with coordinates would be adding to
    that index.

    Filtered at the conversion, not at the log. The ward CSV on the card
    keeps every device the radio heard; only what leaves for a public
    database is trimmed, and the conversion reports how many rows it dropped.
    Withholding from WiGLE and lying to the operator about what their own
    radio heard are different things.

    Spotter never fed the WiGLE export in the first place - it writes its own
    /spotter_<ms>.csv with no coordinates in it, and gps.cpp had no reference
    to Spotter before this. The exposure was the wardriver logging every BLE
    device it hears by name, MAC and position, which it does for a KARR
    module the same as for a doorbell.

  * check_spotter_oui.py: 151 checks to 197. A short prefix must declare a
    length or be listed with the reason it does not need one; an exemption
    fails once the row it was written for is gone; isVehicleName has to exist,
    select on Kind::Vehicle, and be called from gps.cpp.

    Two of those checks were wrong when first written and were fixed rather
    than kept. A bare length threshold failed on {"Flock", 0}, which is a
    false positive - a distinctive word is not a two-letter prefix - so an
    exemption list replaced the threshold instead of the threshold being
    tuned until it passed. And the gps.cpp check read `"Spotter::isVehicleName"
    in gps`, which the comment beside the call satisfied on its own; it now
    requires the name followed by an open paren.

  * The Spotter screen render gains a KARR row. It is also the first orange
    row in it: the grading has three levels and the render showed two.

0.3.1 - 2026-09-19
------------------

Spotter hears Axon hardware on Bluetooth as well as WiFi.

  * The OUI table was matched against WiFi source addresses only. The BLE
    side matched on company ID, service UUID and name prefix and never
    looked at the address, so a unit advertising over BLE and not WiFi went
    past unnamed. github.com/PoliceDetector/PoliceDetector is a 60-line
    script that does nothing but that one match - Axon's block, 00:25:DF,
    against BLE addresses - and it was the thing this had missed. The
    signature itself was already here and already graded Strong.

  * Strong entries only, and only on a public address.

    Strong is the two blocks IEEE assigned to the vendors themselves, Flock
    Safety and Axon. The other 34 are contract manufacturers and module
    vendors - 23 Liteon, 2 Espressif, Qualcomm Atheros - and on WiFi they
    are graded Weak and read as corroboration for an SSID or a beacon. On
    BLE a scan hears every advertiser in the room rather than only the ones
    probing for a network, so Espressif's block would report a shelf of dev
    boards as surveillance hardware, and the board running the scan is one.

    The public-address gate is not caution for its own sake. A random
    address's top two bits encode the address type, not a vendor, and
    00:25:DF has top two bits 00 - the shape of a non-resolvable private
    address. The strongest entry in the table is also the one a randomised
    address could wear by chance. One in 16.7 million per rotation, and the
    gate costs a comparison.

  * tools/check_spotter_oui.py, 151 checks. Conf::Strong now does two jobs:
    it grades the finding and it decides what is allowed on BLE, and nothing
    in C++ connects the second to the first. Promoting a Liteon block while
    thinking only about how a WiFi hit is labelled would put it on the BLE
    path silently. The check fails on that, on either gate being removed
    from Spotter.cpp, and on a new Conf level added above Strong - each
    verified by making the change and watching it fail.

  * Not copied from the same script: its threshold counts sightings rather
    than devices and the counter does not reset, so one camera seen five
    times in 35 seconds reads as a raid. That is the failure the
    tracker-follow maturity gate exists to avoid.

Verified in the built image rather than the source: the BLE callback now
makes two getAddress calls, one memcmp and three record calls, against one,
none and two before, and the entry filter is visible as a branch on the conf
byte. -28 bytes of flash, which is layout noise rather than a claim that
code was removed; RAM unchanged.

0.2.9 - 2026-09-19
------------------

A detector for trackers that rotate their identity to avoid being detected.

Bluetooth:

  * New: AirTag Sniffer reports a rotating tail. It keyed its rows on the
    BLE address, which is fine against a real AirTag and useless against a
    clone - the published ESP32 one cycles a couple of thousand public keys,
    so every rotation made a fresh row, the hit count reset, and after 32
    identities the list started evicting. The harder something worked to
    avoid detection, the less it stood out.

  * ESP32-DIV/TrackerFollow.{h,cpp} asks a different question: has something
    been beside me, at the same distance, wearing a succession of names.
    Sightings of the long form of Continuity 0x12 go into RSSI lanes; a lane
    held without a long gap while collecting persistent identities fast
    enough is the finding. No BLE, no display, no clock of its own, so it is
    tested on a host.

  * Two gates, both added because a scripted scenario failed rather than
    because they sounded sensible:

      A shopping centre read as a tail. A lane is not "one device holding
      station", it is "anything at roughly this distance", and in a crowd
      every lane stays occupied by a parade of strangers. Fixed by counting
      only identities seen at least three times - a follower's keys persist,
      a passer-by is seen once.

      Sitting still crept into an alert. Half an hour beside your own AirTag
      reaches two identities and forty-five minutes reaches three, which was
      the Weak threshold. Fixed with a rate gate: a qualifying lane averages
      a new persistent identity at least every four minutes. Apple rotates
      at about 0.07 a minute, the clone at 2.0.

  * Measured margins, not estimates: desk at 45 min 0.07/min and silent;
    concourse nothing matured at all; clone following 2.00/min and reported
    within two minutes. There is a check that three hours at a desk is as
    quiet as thirty. 315 checks in tools/check_tracker_follow.py.

  * On screen: a tail line between the header and the columns, always shown,
    because "no tail" is the answer the feature exists to give. Rows in the
    implicated lane get a red bar on the RIGHT edge - the left edge is the
    selection bar. Churn is on the line and above 20 new identities a minute
    the verdict draws orange rather than red, because a crowd degrades every
    signal here at once. Costs the list one row, 10 down to 9. Addresses are
    hashed before the detector sees them.

  * docs/pueo/tracker-follow.md records what it cannot do, including the
    weakness named by arXiv:2602.07656 (AirCatch): RSSI is a function of
    transmit power and transmit power is the adversary's to choose. A clone
    that randomises its output level alongside its keys never holds a lane
    and this reports nothing. Their answer - fingerprinting the transmitter
    by carrier frequency offset - is better and is not implementable here: a
    NimBLE advertisement report carries no phase, no I/Q and no frequency
    offset, which is why their work ships a separate micro-SDR.

Corrected:

  * docs/pueo/spoofers.md said AirTag Spoofer sends offline finding. It does
    not. buildProximityPacket() emits Continuity 0x07 proximity pairing with
    prefix 0x05 - the setup popup for an unpaired tag. Offline finding is
    0x12, which nothing in this firmware transmits; the only code that
    touches it is AirTagSniffer, which receives it. My error in the 0.2.4
    write-up.

Wardriving:

  * WiGLE uploads identify as Pueo. The CSV header said model=ESP32-DIV,
    brand=ESP32-DIV and the User-Agent said ESP32-DIV-Wardrive/1, so every
    upload from this build was filed against the wrong firmware. appRelease
    and release now come from PUEO_VERSION rather than a hardcoded 1.0.
    Verified against the built image rather than the source.

+1364 bytes of flash, +1168 of RAM.

0.2.8 - 2026-09-19
------------------

Address rotation fails loudly instead of silently.

  * bleRotateAddressNow() restarted advertising when the address change
    failed, but never checked whether the restart itself succeeded. If a
    board dislikes the stop/set/start the symptom would have been a spoofer
    that mysteriously stops transmitting, once a second, with nothing said
    on screen and nothing in the log.

  * Each step is now checked and named: "stop refused", "set addr refused",
    "restart refused". Three consecutive failures latch rotation off and put
    one line on screen saying which step it was.

  * Advertising is restarted whatever happened in between. Silently going
    dark is worse than a stale address, and a stale address is what every
    release before 0.2.7 did anyway.

  * Entering a spoofer resets the latch, so a transient refusal does not
    disable rotation for the rest of the session.

+760 bytes.

0.2.7 - 2026-09-19
------------------

The BLE spoofers stop transmitting from one fixed address.

Bluetooth:

  * BLE Spoofer, Sour Apple and AirTag Spoofer now rotate their BLE address
    once a second. All three built a random address and then dropped it on
    the floor - nothing called esp_ble_gap_set_rand_addr, setOwnAddrType or
    ble_hs_id_set_rnd - so every packet went out from the ESP32's one fixed
    address. A stream of a dozen different Apple products sharing an address
    is not a disguise, and it made the transmitting device attributable for
    as long as it ran.

    This was documented rather than fixed in 0.2.4, because making the
    spoofers harder to attribute is an increase in what they do rather than
    a correction to what they claim. That is the owner's call and it has now
    been made.

  * Non-resolvable private, not static random. ble_hs_id_gen_rnd(1, ...)
    asks the stack for an NRPA, which is the address type the specification
    defines for exactly this; a static random address is meant to hold still
    for a power cycle, so rotating one is out of spec even though the
    controller allows it. Letting the stack generate it also sidesteps the
    top-two-bits rule, which the old dead code got right once (0xC0) and
    wrong twice - 0xF0, and on the wrong end of the array for NimBLE, which
    takes the address little-endian.

  * Once a second rather than once a burst, because the controller refuses
    HCI_LE_Set_Random_Address while advertising is enabled, so a rotation is
    stop, set, start. AirTagSpoofer carries a note that repeated stop/start
    was resetting the board, so rotating every 40 ms burst is the exact
    pattern to avoid. kBleAddrRotateMs is the dial if the board turns out to
    tolerate more. On a failure advertising restarts on the old address
    rather than staying dark.

  * AirTag Spoofer's address display reads the stack instead of a kept copy.
    NimBLEDevice::getAddress() prefers the PUBLIC address and only falls back
    to random when there is no public one, which on an ESP32 never happens,
    so it would have gone back to confidently showing the one address the
    radio is not transmitting from. ble_hs_id_copy_addr(BLE_ADDR_RANDOM, ...)
    gets the real one.

+504 bytes. The four NimBLE symbols are in the linked image, checked with nm
rather than assumed - including ble_hs_hci_util_set_random_addr, which is
what actually issues the HCI command.

0.2.5 - 2026-09-19
------------------

Google's side of the BLE world, which this device had never looked at, plus
a Flipper .sub reader and two more advertisement templates.

Bluetooth:

  * New feature: Fast Pair, on page 1 of the Bluetooth menu. Two halves that
    are deliberately not the same act.

    Listening is passive - a BLE scan with active scanning off, reading the
    0xFE2C service data every Fast Pair device broadcasts unasked.

    Probing transmits. It opens a GATT connection to ONE device the operator
    selected and writes 80 bytes to the key-based pairing characteristic, to
    test for CVE-2025-36911. It sits behind a confirm screen that names the
    target address and states what will be sent, it never runs on its own,
    and it never touches more than the selected row. Point it at your own
    devices or ones you have permission to test.

  * ESP32-DIV/FastPair.{h,cpp} parses the advertisement. Fast Pair lives in
    BLE service data, not manufacturer data, which is why the three
    Apple-shaped parsers already here were structurally incapable of seeing
    one.

    A payload is a 24-bit Model ID only when it is exactly three bytes. Every
    other length is a not-discoverable frame - flags, account key filter,
    optional salt and battery - with no Model ID anywhere in it. Reading its
    first three bytes as one produces a confident, plausible, meaningless
    number, and since most earbuds already belong to somebody that is the
    common case rather than the edge case.

  * Nothing correlates devices across address rotation, because Fast Pair
    deliberately offers nothing to correlate on. A Model ID names a model,
    not a unit, so two strangers with the same earbuds share one. An account
    key filter is a salted bloom filter whose salt rotates precisely so a
    passive listener cannot follow the device. Two tests assert those
    non-properties, so adding correlation later means deleting a test that
    says why not.

  * The model-name table is empty on purpose. Google's mapping needs an API
    key, and the lists circulating for "fast pair spam" tools are IDs chosen
    to provoke a popup, with names that are often wrong. An unknown ID prints
    as six hex digits, which is true.

  * BLE Spoofer gains Microsoft Swift Pair and Flipper Zero, device types 22
    and 23. Both built from primary sources.

    Swift Pair sends sub scenario 0x00, pairing over LE only. The other two
    values the spec defines describe a dual-mode peripheral and 0x01 also
    requires a BR/EDR address in the same advertisement; this has none, so
    sending either would advertise a capability that is not there. Several
    spam tools send 0x03, which the spec's table does not define at all. The
    display name is Pueo-XXXX rather than a real product's, so the operator
    can tell their own notification from a stranger's.

    Flipper uses service UUID 0x3080 with the hardware colour in the low
    bits - from Flipper's own serial profile, not from captures - so
    0x3081-0x3083 are one service on differently coloured hardware.

  * Found, not changed: devices_uuid is handed to addServiceUUID()
    immediately before setAdvertisementData(), which replaces the whole
    payload, so it never reaches the air. It also carries 9000 where the
    Bluetooth base UUID has 8000. Removing it should change nothing, but
    that is analysis rather than a measurement.

Sub-GHz:

  * ESP32-DIV/SubFile.{h,cpp} reads Flipper .sub captures. Pueo's own export
    is a binary blob with a magic number that nothing else can read; .sub is
    what the rest of the sub-GHz world trades in.

    RAW files are refused by name, three ways - Filetype, Protocol: RAW, and
    RAW_Data. They share the key file's header and carry microsecond timings
    where the key would be, and a SubGhzProfile has nowhere to put timings,
    so half-parsing one yields a profile that looks valid and transmits
    nonsense.

    Only Princeton claims an rc-switch protocol number, because Princeton is
    PT2262/EV1527 and rc-switch protocol 1 is the same thing. CAME, NICE
    FLO, Holtek and the rest parse and return protocol 0 with the name kept.
    Their timings do not match an rc-switch number and a guess would
    transmit something subtly wrong while presenting as correct.

  * Nothing calls the .sub reader yet. Wiring it in is a UI change -
    saveProfile() reads globals rather than taking a profile, and there are
    only five EEPROM slots, so an import has to ask which to replace.

Checked:

  * tools/check_fastpair.py, 80,111 checks. Every fuzz input asserts
    (frame == ModelId) == (len == 3), which is the whole rule in one line.
  * tools/check_fastpair_probe.py, 50,293 checks, most of them on the
    provider address at bytes 2..7. That field is the test: a probe broken
    so subtly that every device reports "no response" is indistinguishable
    from a room full of correctly-behaving devices.
  * tools/check_sub_parse.py, 60,891 checks, including every single-byte
    mutation of a valid file.
  * tools/check_ble_adv.py, 1,095 checks. Walks each advertisement the way a
    receiver does and insists the walk lands exactly on the packet's end - a
    wrong AD length byte still transmits, still looks right in the log, and
    is silently dropped by everything that hears it. It also walks the three
    older fixed templates, which had never been checked; all are well
    formed. The Google one decodes as a discoverable Fast Pair frame
    advertising model ID 00B727, one fixed model, never varied.

Fixed while writing the above:

  * mbedtls here is 2.28, so the 3.x MBEDTLS_PRIVATE field accessor was
    wrong.
  * deriveKey() called mbedtls_ecp_point_init instead of _free in its
    cleanup, leaking a point per probe.

Still true: none of this has run on hardware. The Fast Pair probe's crypto
path in particular has never derived a key against a real provider. Treat
the first run on real hardware as the first test of it.

0.2.4 - 2026-09-19
------------------

Mostly reading what features already claimed, and fixing what turned out
not to be true.

WiFi:

  * The promiscuous filter is global and sticky, and nothing set it back.
    Two features asked for management frames only, so Packet Monitor - which
    wants data frames, because it writes them to its pcap and counts them on
    screen - saw management frames only if the captive portal or Karma had
    run first in the same session. No error, no empty file, just a capture
    missing everything that was not a beacon. Every feature that reads
    frames now states what it wants each time it starts.
  * Packet Monitor recognises WPA handshakes in the frames it was already
    recording. ESP32-DIV/Eapol.{h,cpp} finds the 802.1X payload by computing
    the header length from the frame control field rather than testing a
    fixed offset, so it still works on a frame carrying a fourth address or
    an HT Control field; the four messages are told apart by three bits of
    the Key Information field, plus the Key Type bit so a group rekey is not
    mistaken for M3. The header shows how many networks have M2 and M3.
  * Forcing a handshake by deauthenticating a client is deliberately NOT
    wired up. The decision logic exists, disarms on capture and after six
    bursts, and nothing calls it - Packet Monitor runs in WIFI_MODE_NULL and
    cannot transmit anyway. See docs/pueo/eapol-capture.md.

Bluetooth:

  * AirTag Sniffer can tell a separated tag from somebody's phone. Apple
    uses type 0x12 for both the long form that carries the rotating public
    key, which a device broadcasts once separated from its owner, and a
    short online status ping that every participating iPhone and Mac emits.
    They differ in the length byte, which the parser had and never read.
    Battery and the maintained flag are decoded from the status byte, which
    was being stored and never shown. Scanning is passive now: an AirTag
    advertises non-connectable, so the scan requests bought nothing.
  * BLE Scanner drew manufacturer and service data as text, with
    String((char*)raw.c_str()), which stops at the first zero byte. Apple's
    company identifier is 4C 00, so every Apple device displayed as the
    single letter L. Both are hex now, capped to the row width. Tx Power is
    guarded by haveTXPower() like the fields below it, rather than printing
    whatever was left in the field.
  * Skimmer Detect's signatures carry a Radio column. It scans BLE only, so
    eleven of its twenty-eight names can never match, including seven of the
    eight rated 5 - HC-05 and HC-06 among them. Kept and marked rather than
    removed. The table's order is load-bearing and now says so.
  * Sniffer's two alerts are annotated rather than changed. Jamming
    Suspected fires on any device seen more than twenty times, a counter
    that is never reset, so about two seconds of a normal beacon. MAC
    Spoofing Suspected cannot detect a MAC change at all: the row is found
    by matching that exact address. See docs/pueo/ble-sniffer.md.

Sub-GHz:

  * One frequency list instead of two identical copies in different
    namespaces, which nothing kept in step.

Tooling:

  * tools/check_eapol.py and tools/check_airtag_parse.py, modelling the
    parsers against synthetic frames behind a buffer that refuses any read
    outside them.

    device rather than to that probe, which leaves the SSID and the channel
    out deliberately.
  * One device is one row when it changes address. A new address folds into
    an existing row on a fingerprint match, but only when both addresses
    are locally administered: two cameras of one model share an element
    set, and merging on the fingerprint alone would report one where there
    are two.
  * Rows show whether the address is made up, how many times it has
    changed, and how long the device has been in range.

    three channel groups, which reads as twelve channels across three
    radios and was neither: the board profile maps all three chip selects
    onto one module, and the setup loop called startConstCarrier once per
    channel with each call replacing the last. The hopping that did work
    was in the feature loops, picking a random channel every iteration and
    writing it three times. Now round-robin on a 4 ms dwell, written once.
  * onPacket no longer discards wildcard probe requests. A zero-length SSID

    those frames carry.

Tooling:
